# Suggesting changes

This handbook only works if the people it governs can change it. Anyone who works in the lab can propose an edit, a new section, or the removal of something that does not work.

## Ways to propose a change

Open a pull request if you are comfortable with GitHub. Open an issue if you would rather describe the problem and let someone else write the fix. If you do not use GitHub, email the lab head, or give your suggestion to the external contact described in chapter 5, who passes it on without your name. Every route gets the same hearing.

## What happens next

Small fixes, such as typos, broken links and clearer wording that does not change meaning, are merged by the lab head within a week.

Anything that changes a rule or an expectation is discussed at a lab meeting first. That includes working hours, authorship, data handling, safety, concerns procedures, and the expectations of any role. The lab head merges the change after the discussion, or says in writing why not. Changes that affect members are announced at the next lab meeting and recorded in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md).

## Writing style

Write plainly. Say who does what and by when. Use "must" for requirements and "should" for strong defaults, and do not mix them up. Keep one rule per sentence. If a sentence could appear in any lab's handbook, ask whether it needs to be here.

Do not add experimental protocols or instrument instructions. Those change faster than a handbook and belong with the projects that use them.

## Public repository

Do not commit private phone numbers, door codes, passwords, internal chat links, personal details about members, unpublished results, material covered by a partner agreement, or anything about a specific concern or dispute. Keep those in the lab's private space and refer to them from the handbook by description.
