# Co-writing workshop

The first draft comes from the lab head. A handbook the whole group has shaped gets used more and argued about less. Run this with the first members in their first month, then use it again at each review.

## Format

Ninety minutes, everyone in the room including the lab head.

1. Ten minutes: the lab head says what is in the draft and what is unfinished, and says out loud that anything in it can be changed.
2. Fifteen minutes: everyone answers three or four of the questions below on their own, in writing.
3. Thirty minutes: small groups of two or three compare answers and mark where the draft says something they would not follow in practice, or leaves something out.
4. Twenty minutes: the whole group goes through the marked points. Agree what changes, what stays, and what needs more thought.
5. Fifteen minutes: assign each change to an owner and a date.

Run it away from the bench if you can. The lab head should speak last in each discussion.

## Questions

1. What does good science look like in this lab, in practice, day to day?
2. Describe a week when everyone is working well together. What are people doing?
3. What did you wish someone had told you in the first week of any lab you have worked in?
4. What working hours are reasonable here? What would make you think you were expected to work more than that?
5. When do you hesitate to ask a question, and what would make it easier?
6. Which decisions should one person make, and which should the group make?
7. How do you want to hear about authorship, and when?
8. Which concerns would you find hard to raise, and who would you trust with them?
9. What support would you want for a career outside the lab's own field?
10. What should the lab never do?
11. What belongs in this handbook, and what belongs in protocols or somewhere else?
12. What in the draft made you stop reading?

## After the workshop

The lab head makes the agreed changes within two weeks and circulates the result. Anything that was not agreed goes on the agenda of the next lab meeting. Record the date and the main changes in the changelog.
