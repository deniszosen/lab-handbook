# About the lab and this handbook

Zosen Lab is a research group led by Denis Zosen. We study how the human nervous system develops and how medicines and environmental chemicals can change it, using human stem cell-derived tissue and cell models. This handbook says how we work together: what each role can expect and is expected to do, how decisions about time, credit and data get made, and what to do when something goes wrong.

It does not replace your contract, the rules of the institution that employs or hosts you, or the law. Where those say something different, they win and we fix the handbook. Where this handbook is more specific than they are, or asks for more than the minimum, follow the handbook.

Protocols, instrument manuals and analysis pipelines are not here. They change faster than a handbook should, and they belong with the projects that use them.

## Where the lab sits

The lab is hosted at the Norwegian Centre for Molecular Biosciences and Medicine (NCMBM), University of Oslo. Employment, the formal supervision of degrees, health and safety, and ethics approvals run through the institution that employs or enrols you and through the host. This handbook sits on top of those rules. The lab head's accountability in this handbook covers the lab's science and how the group works. It does not replace the duties the law gives your institution.

## A new lab

The lab is new, and some of what this handbook describes is still being set up: the chore rota, the inventory register, the alumni list and the anonymous survey. Several practices assume five or more people. Where a rule works differently in a smaller group, the chapter says so.

## Who it applies to

Everyone who works in the lab: staff, postdocs, PhD students, MSc, BSc and exchange students, technicians, interns, guests, and the lab head. Visiting researchers and external collaborators are covered by the chapters on conduct, safety, data and authorship.

The lab head is held to the same text. If the lab head breaks it, you can say so, and [chapter 5](05-conduct-and-raising-concerns.md) explains how.

## How to read it

In your first week, read chapters 2, 3, 4, 5 and 12. They cover values, your role, working hours and communication, conduct and how to raise a concern, and safety. In your first month, read chapters 6 to 9 on mentoring, research practice, data and authorship. Use the rest as reference.

Then sit down with the lab head and fill in the [expectations agreement](../templates/expectations-agreement.md). The handbook gives the general rules. The agreement records what applies to you.

## How it was written

The lab head drafted version 0.1 using published guidance on lab handbooks and examples from other groups (see [credits](../CREDITS.md)). A handbook that only the boss writes gets less buy-in than one the whole group writes. So the first members of the lab revise this draft in their first month, using the [co-writing workshop](../maintainers/co-writing-workshop.md), and what they agree goes into version 1.0.

After that the group reviews the handbook twice a year, as described in [chapter 15](15-keeping-this-handbook-alive.md).
