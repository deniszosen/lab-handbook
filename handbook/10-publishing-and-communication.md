# Publishing and communication

## Publishing

Each project has a publication plan in its README: what the paper is, the target audience, a first-choice journal, and a rough date. The plan is reviewed at the start of every project and whenever the story changes.

Post a preprint when a paper is ready for submission, unless a patent filing, a partner agreement or a journal's rules say otherwise. Choose open access routes when funds allow, and follow your funder's requirements. Choose journals for fit and for a sound review process. Do not publish in journals that charge fees without real peer review.

Submit only when every author has read and approved the final version. The corresponding author keeps the correspondence and shares decisions with all authors within two working days.

Acknowledge funders, facilities and people who helped. Use the affiliation your institution requires on each paper, and make sure it is the same across the lab's papers.

## Peer review

Members of the lab are encouraged to review papers, and early-career members may co-review with the lab head when the journal allows it, with the editor told. Reviews are confidential. Do not discuss a manuscript you are reviewing outside the review.

## Talks and posters

Talks and posters give credit to everyone whose work is shown, by name on the slide. Do not show unpublished data from other lab members or from collaborators without their permission. Check the lab's agreements with partners before showing work done with them.

## Social media and public statements

You can talk about your own work and your own views. Say clearly when you speak for yourself and not for the lab. Do not post unpublished results, partner data, photos of colleagues without their consent, or anything about patients or study participants. Do not answer press enquiries on the lab's behalf. Pass them to the lab head, who will coordinate with the institution's communications office.

## The lab website and public engagement

The lab website lists current members, projects, publications, preprints, datasets and code. People can opt out of having a photo or profile on the website, and nobody is asked to justify it. Public engagement and outreach are encouraged and count as work. Plan events with the lab head and the institution's communications team.

## Confidentiality

Unpublished results, partner and sponsor information, patient or participant data, and anything covered by a confidentiality agreement are confidential. Keep them off public platforms and out of personal messages, and discuss them only with people who are entitled to know. If you are not sure whether something is confidential, ask before you share it.
