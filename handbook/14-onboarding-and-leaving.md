# Onboarding and leaving

## Joining

The full list is in the [onboarding checklist](../templates/onboarding-checklist.md). In outline:

Before your first day, the lab head sends you a welcome note with your start time, who will meet you, and what to bring. Your accounts, access and workspace are prepared, and a named buddy from the lab is assigned to you.

On your first day you meet the group, tour the lab and its emergency equipment, and get your accounts working.

In your first week you read the chapters listed in [chapter 1](01-about-the-lab.md), complete the safety induction, meet the lab head for a first one-to-one, and fill in the [expectations agreement](../templates/expectations-agreement.md).

In your first month you start your first project plan, complete required training (including cell culture sign-off before you culture cells on your own), meet the people you will work with most, and give the lab head your first round of feedback on the onboarding itself. You also read this handbook critically and suggest changes.

At three months you review progress and the expectations agreement with the lab head, and adjust both.

Exchange and Erasmus+ students and visiting researchers also bring the agreement from their home institution before day one. The lab head confirms the dates and the deliverables with them in writing.

## Leaving

Start planning at least four weeks before you go, or as soon as you know your last day. The [offboarding checklist](../templates/offboarding-checklist.md) lists the steps. In outline:

- agree what will be handed over, to whom, and by when
- hand over data, notebooks, code, samples and inventories, and check that someone else can find and use them
- finish or hand over the analyses and drafts you own, and agree the authorship plan for work that is still unpublished
- return keys, cards and equipment, close accounts, and transfer ownership of shared files
- agree how you will be contacted about questions on your work
- have an exit conversation with the lab head, where you can say what worked and what did not in the lab

Your records stay with the lab. You keep a copy of your own thesis or contributions if your institution and any partner agreements allow it.

## Staying in touch

You are welcome to stay in the lab's alumni list. The lab head will keep writing references for you, and you remain an author on work you contributed to. If you leave on bad terms, you still have the rights described in [chapter 9](09-authorship-and-credit.md), and the routes in [chapter 5](05-conduct-and-raising-concerns.md) remain open to you.
