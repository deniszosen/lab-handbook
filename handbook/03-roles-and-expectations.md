# Roles and expectations

Each role gets two lists: what you can expect from the lab, and what the lab expects from you. If your situation does not fit a role, talk to the lab head and the two of you will write down what applies to you.

## Everyone

You can expect a written expectations agreement in your first two weeks, a named person to ask when you are stuck, specific and timely feedback, safe working conditions, and credit for what you contribute.

We expect you to follow the chapters on conduct, safety, data and authorship, to record your work so others can follow it, and to come to the weekly lab meeting unless you are on leave or have a clash with teaching or clinical duties. Tell the lab head early when a deadline or commitment is at risk. Take your share of housekeeping.

## Lab head

Denis Zosen leads the lab, sets its scientific direction, raises its funding, and answers for how the group works and for the science it produces. Your institution keeps the duties the law gives it as your employer or as the host of your degree.

The lab head commits to:

- a one-to-one with each member at least every two weeks
- returning written drafts within ten working days, and saying in advance when it will take longer
- replying to messages within two working days
- discussing authorship at the start of every project and again as the project changes
- not asking anyone to work outside normal hours, except in an emergency
- supporting careers in and outside academia, and writing references on request
- acting on concerns raised under [chapter 5](05-conduct-and-raising-concerns.md)
- asking the group for feedback twice a year, anonymously once there are five or more members, and reporting back what changed

The lab head's outside roles and interests are disclosed as described in [chapter 11](11-ethics-integrity-and-compliance.md). If you think the lab head is not meeting any of this, say so. Chapter 5 shows how.

## Postdocs

You can expect a project with real room for your own ideas, an authorship plan agreed at the start, protected time for fellowship and grant applications, the chance to supervise junior members with training for it, and support to move on to independence or to a career outside academia.

We expect you to drive your project, help train newer members, and write up what you find. Supervising students is agreed with the lab head beforehand and counted in your workload. If you want to take a line of work with you when you leave, that is negotiated early, written down, and follows institutional and funder rules.

## PhD students

You can expect a project that can be finished within the funded period, a supervision team of the lab head plus at least one other mentor, with the formal supervision arranged through the host department, a development plan reviewed every year, and support to finish. You will be first author on work you lead. Your contract sets your hours and any teaching load, and nobody should expect more.

We expect you to own your thesis project, write short progress notes regularly, tell your supervisors when you are stuck, meet your programme's milestones, and attend required courses.

## MSc, BSc and exchange students

You can expect a bounded project with a named day-to-day supervisor, training before you work independently, a plan that fits your thesis timeline, and honest feedback on drafts. You will not be asked to do unpaid work outside your thesis project. If you meet the criteria in [chapter 9](09-authorship-and-credit.md), you will be an author. Exchange and Erasmus+ students also bring the agreement from their home institution. Your expectations agreement lists its deliverables and dates, and the lab head and your coordinator are told early if either changes.

We expect you to follow your supervisor's instructions on safety, ask before doing anything new, keep a notebook, and hand over your data and code when you finish.

## Technicians and research staff

You can expect your expertise to be treated as expertise. You will be involved in planning the experiments you run. You get authorship where the criteria are met and a proper acknowledgement where they are not. You will have named areas of responsibility, and opportunities for training and certification. Being the person who knows where things are does not make you the default for errands.

We expect you to keep equipment and stock in working order, maintain the quality of the protocols you own, flag problems early, write down procedures, and train others.

## Visiting researchers and guests

You can expect an induction on safety and data rules, a named host in the lab, and clear terms about what is shared and how credit will work.

We expect you to stay within the agreed project, follow lab rules, and hand over data and records when you leave.

## External collaborators

You can expect a written agreement on data, materials, intellectual property and publication before work starts, and fair credit for your contribution.

We expect the same in return, and that you do not pass the lab's unpublished results to anyone else without consent.
