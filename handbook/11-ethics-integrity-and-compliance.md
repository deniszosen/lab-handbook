# Ethics, integrity and compliance

This chapter sets out the lab's principles. Contracts, institutional policy and law decide the details, and when they differ from this chapter they win.

## Research integrity

We are honest about what we did and found, open about how we did it, careful in how we do it, and fair to colleagues and to the people and animals our work involves. Fabrication, falsification and plagiarism are research misconduct. So are hiding relevant data, misrepresenting contributions, and retaliating against a person who reports a concern. Questionable practices, such as selective reporting, choosing an analysis after seeing the result, or sloppy records, are not misconduct in the formal sense, and we treat them as problems to fix.

If you suspect misconduct, use the routes in [chapter 5](05-conduct-and-raising-concerns.md).

## Approvals come first

No work starts until the approvals it needs are in place and a copy is in the project folder. Depending on the project, that can include:

- ethics approval for research on people, patient data or human biological material (in Norway, the regional committees for medical and health research ethics, REK)
- consent and approval covering human cell lines, stem cell lines and other donor-derived material, which may fall under the rules for health research and biobanks (in Norway, the Health Research Act and the Biobank Act)
- approval for animal work, including a check on whether the embryo stage you plan to use needs it (in Norway, from the Norwegian Food Safety Authority, Mattilsynet)
- approval for work with genetically modified organisms, and a check with the biosafety officer on whether genome-edited cells or viral vectors need it (in Norway, under the Gene Technology Act)
- biosafety clearance for the containment level of the work
- data protection assessment and agreements where personal data are involved

Outside Norway, the local equivalents apply. If you do not know what a new line of work needs, ask the lab head before you start.

## Conflicts of interest

A conflict of interest exists when a financial or personal interest could reasonably appear to influence your work in the lab. Having one is not wrong. Hiding one is.

Tell the lab head about outside work that touches the lab's field, including consulting, advisory roles, board seats, company ownership, and paid review work, and tell your institution as its rules require. Do not use the lab's equipment, samples, data, funds or people for outside work without a written agreement. Do not ask students or staff to do work for an outside venture as part of their lab duties, and do not pressure anyone to take part in one.

The lab head's own outside roles and interests are disclosed in writing to every member of the lab and to the host institution, updated at least once a year, and shared with anyone who asks. Interests that touch the lab's field are also declared in papers, grant applications and talks, as each venue requires.

Disclose relevant interests in papers, grant applications and talks as the venue requires.

## Intellectual property

Who owns an invention or a dataset depends on employment contracts, institutional policy, funder terms and law, such as the Norwegian Act on the Right to Employees' Inventions. If you think you have made something that could be patented, tell the lab head before you publish it, post a preprint, or present it. Disclosing early can protect rights. Disclosing late can destroy them. Inventorship follows who contributed to the invention, in the same way that authorship follows who contributed to the paper.

Keep clear records of when and how ideas and results were produced.

## Working with industry and other partners

Sign the agreement before data or materials move. It should cover what is shared, who owns what, what each side can use, confidentiality, and publication rights. Material transfer agreements are needed for biological materials. The lab does not accept terms that would stop a student from graduating or a member from publishing their own work after a reasonable review period, and members are told what the terms of any project they work on are before they start.

Partners are held to the same standards on data, credit and conduct as members of the lab.
