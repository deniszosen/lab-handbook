# Conduct and raising concerns

## Standards

We treat each other with respect. Disagree about the science as hard as you like, and keep it about the work.

The following is not tolerated in the lab, at lab events, at conferences, on partner sites, or online in lab channels:

- harassment of any kind, including sexual harassment
- discrimination based on gender, ethnicity, religion, disability, sexual orientation, age, or any other protected ground
- bullying, including public humiliation, shouting, persistent exclusion, and giving someone impossible tasks to make them look bad
- unwanted physical contact
- sharing someone's private information without their consent
- retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith

A romantic or sexual relationship between a supervisor and anyone they supervise, assess or influence the career of is not compatible with that supervision. Tell the lab head as early as you can, or the department head or HR if the relationship involves the lab head, so that supervision can be rearranged. Telling early is never held against you.

Lab events are open to everyone. Drinking is optional and never expected.

## How to raise a concern

Concerns range from "something someone said bothered me" to "I think data have been falsified". You pick the route that feels safe. In rough order of formality:

1. Talk to the person directly, if that feels safe and you want to.
2. Talk to the lab head, in person or by email.
3. Go to someone outside the lab: the head of your department or institute, your HR contact, your union representative, or your institution's ombud or student welfare service. Safety concerns can go to the safety representative (verneombud). You can skip step 2 and go here directly, and you must if the concern is about the lab head. Students and visitors can also go to the coordinator, supervisor or ombud at their home institution.
4. Use a formal channel: your institution's whistleblowing (varsling) route or its research integrity procedure. Serious breaches of research integrity also have a national route.
5. Use the external contact. At onboarding, the lab head gives every member the name of at least one senior person outside the lab, such as a department head or an experienced researcher at another institution, whom they can approach about anything concerning the lab head. This matters most for guests and others with no institutional home of their own. The person has agreed to take on the role and knows what it involves. The lab keeps their name and contact details in its private space.

If the concern is urgent or someone is in danger, use the emergency numbers in [chapter 12](12-safety.md).

## What happens when we receive a concern

We acknowledge it within three working days. We ask what you want to happen and explain what the options are.

We keep it as confidential as we can. If law, safety or institutional rules mean it has to go further, we tell you before it does.

We report to the institution whenever its rules require.

Retaliation is treated as seriously as the conduct that was reported.

We tell you the outcome as far as confidentiality allows, and check in with you a month later.

The lab head keeps a short factual record, visible only to those who need it.

## Conflicts between members

Most conflicts are about time, credit or communication. Start by talking to each other. If that has not settled it after two weeks, both people meet the lab head together. If the lab head is part of the conflict, a neutral senior person from outside the lab mediates. Authorship disputes follow [chapter 9](09-authorship-and-credit.md).

## Concerns about the lab head

The lab head is bound by this handbook. A concern about the lab head goes through step 3 or 5 above. The lab head will not try to find out who raised it, will not ask other members to take sides, and will cooperate with whatever process follows.
