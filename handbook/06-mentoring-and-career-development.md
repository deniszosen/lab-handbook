# Mentoring and career development

## Supervision

Every member has a day-to-day contact and at least one mentor besides the lab head. For students, that mentor sits outside the lab or is a second supervisor.

One-to-ones happen every two weeks. Students also write a short progress note each month, and everyone has a longer review every six months with written goals for the next six.

Feedback on drafts comes back within ten working days. If you need it sooner for a deadline, say so when you hand it over. Good feedback is specific, is about the work, and says what is working as well as what to fix. If feedback is unclear, tell the person who gave it. That is their job to fix.

Independence grows with experience. Early on, expect close guidance and ask for it. Later, more decisions are yours, and the lab head still reads your results and challenges your plans.

## Development plans

Within your first two months you write an individual development plan using the [template](../templates/individual-development-plan.md), and you review it with the lab head every year. It covers the skills you want to build, the projects you want to work on, the career directions you are considering, and the support you need. It is your plan, not an assessment of you.

## Careers

The lab prepares people for several paths: academic research, pharma and biotech, clinical research and trial operations, regulatory and medical affairs, venture and consulting, and science policy. Planning to leave academia is a normal plan here, and it carries no penalty.

The lab head will make introductions where possible, read CVs and applications, and run interview practice. Time for career development is working time. Up to five working days a year can go to courses, visits to employers, internships and career events, agreed in advance.

## Fellowships and grants

If you want your own funding, such as a postdoctoral or mobility fellowship, the lab head reads your drafts, arranges a read by someone outside the lab, and counts the writing time as working time. Tell the lab head about deadlines at least two months ahead.

## Teaching and supervising juniors

Supervising a junior member is training for you as the supervisor. It is agreed with the lab head, counted in your workload, and credited when the work is published.

## Conferences, travel and presentations

Present your work outside the lab at least once a year, funds permitting. Travel is planned with the lab head and budgeted in advance.

Nobody is required to travel. You may have reasons of safety, conscience, health, caring duties, visa problems, cost or climate. You only need to tell the lab head, and we will find an alternative: a remote talk, a colleague presenting with full credit to you, or a different event. Not travelling does not count against you.

Give a practice talk at lab meeting before any external presentation.

## References

The lab head writes references on request for current and former members. Ask at least four weeks ahead when you can, and send your CV and a description of the role. The letter will be honest. If the lab head cannot write a strong one for a particular role, they will tell you before writing it so that you can choose another referee.
