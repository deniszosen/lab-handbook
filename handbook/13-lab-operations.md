# Lab operations

## Ordering and budgets

Orders go through the lab's ordering list. Anything over a threshold set by the lab head needs approval before you order. Use the right grant or cost code, and keep receipts and delivery notes. Tell the lab head early when a project is going to need more money than planned.

## Inventory and labelling

Label every tube, plate, box and bottle with your initials, the date and the contents. Anyone who finds unlabelled items in a shared freezer or fridge reports them at the next lab meeting, and the lab head bins them if nobody claims them. Keep the register of strains, lines, antibodies and chemicals current. Each cell line also has a freezer map, a thaw log and a record of its master and working stocks. We audit the freezers and fridges twice a year, and everyone clears out their own material.

## Shared equipment

Book equipment before you use it. Log your use. Clean up afterwards. Report faults the day you find them and put a note on the instrument. Do not repair or modify equipment yourself. Get training from an experienced user before you use an instrument for the first time.

## Core facilities

Shared core facilities, such as sequencing, imaging and flow cytometry, have their own booking, training and cost rules. Book through them, tell the lab head about costs before you start, and acknowledge the facility in papers.

## Shared space and housekeeping

Chores are shared on a rota that includes every member who works in the lab, visiting researchers included. The lab head takes a turn on the chores that cover areas they use. The rota is posted and swaps are fine if you tell the others. Housekeeping is not assigned by seniority, by gender, or to whoever is quietest.

Keep benches tidy and walkways clear. Return shared items. Restock what you use up.

## Visitors

Visitors sign in, are briefed on safety rules, and are not left alone in the lab. A member who brings a visitor is responsible for them.

## Keys and access

Keys and access cards are personal. Do not lend them or copy them. Report a lost one immediately. Return everything when you leave.

## Waste

Sort waste into the correct streams and label it. Chemical, biological and sharps waste each follow institutional rules. If you are unsure which stream something belongs in, ask before you dispose of it.
