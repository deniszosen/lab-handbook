# Working together

## Working hours

We do not expect long hours, and we do not read long hours as dedication. Nobody gets better treatment or more credit for working more. Most experiments fit in a normal day. The ones that do not, such as long incubations, time courses and work with fixed schedules, are planned in advance with the people involved so that nobody gets the late shifts every time. Evening and weekend work happens only when an experiment requires it. It is not routine, and the person who does it takes the time back.

People work different patterns. Agree yours with the lab head in your expectations agreement. Three things have to line up: the weekly lab meeting, your one-to-ones, and the times you need shared equipment or a colleague's help.

Do not send messages that expect an answer outside working hours. If you write at night because it suits you, say in the message that no reply is needed before the next working day, or schedule it to send in the morning.

## Leave and absence

Take your leave. Give the lab head the dates in advance and put them in the shared calendar so experiments can be planned around them. You do not have to explain sick leave beyond what your contract and institution require, and we do not want you working while ill. Parental, caring and other leave follow your institution's rules. The lab adjusts project timelines and deadlines to fit, and leave is never held against anyone when we decide on authorship, references or opportunities.

## Communication

Chat is for quick questions and logistics. Anything that changes a project's direction, a person's duties or a deadline is confirmed by email so it can be found later.

Say what you need and by when. "Can you look at this figure by Thursday?" works. "Any thoughts?" does not.

Expect a reply within two working days. If something is urgent, phone.

Ask science questions in shared channels, not in direct messages, so the answer helps more than one person.

English is the working language of lab meetings and shared documents so that everyone can take part. Side conversations can be in any language.

Unpublished results and partner data stay in the lab's own channels. They do not go into public posts or messages to people outside the lab without the lab head's agreement.

## Meetings

The weekly lab meeting lasts 60 minutes. The first ten are news and logistics. The other fifty are one person's presentation or a group discussion, and presenters rotate. Work in progress is welcome. Polished slides are not required.

One-to-ones with the lab head happen every two weeks and last 30 minutes. Your agenda comes first. A [template](../templates/one-to-one-agenda.md) is available if you want one.

Journal club runs once a month. The presenter picks the paper and circulates it a week ahead.

Whoever leads a meeting sends the agenda 24 hours before and files the notes and actions in the shared folder afterwards. Meetings are held between 09:00 and 16:00 local time so that people with caring duties can attend. Joining online is fine when you need to, and cameras are optional.

## Wellbeing

Research is hard and everyone has bad stretches. The lab head is not a counsellor and does not need to know your health details. What the lab head can do is adjust your workload, deadlines and tasks when you ask. What you say stays private, except where safety, the law or another person's welfare requires otherwise. In that case the lab head tells you first.

If you are struggling, tell the lab head or another person you trust. Your institution's occupational health service and counselling services are there for this. If a colleague seems to be struggling, check in privately, ask how you can help, and avoid guessing at causes.

## Feedback to the lab head

Twice a year the lab asks everyone how the group is working. With five or more members this is a short anonymous survey. In a smaller group nobody can be anonymous, so people send their answers to the external contact described in chapter 5, who passes on a summary without names. The results are discussed at a lab meeting and the lab head says what will change. You can also talk to the lab head privately at any time, or use the routes in [chapter 5](05-conduct-and-raising-concerns.md).
